CompressO 4.1 is here, only a few weeks after 4.0.

This release is about two things. First, **HDR**: CompressO now understands bright HDR photos and videos, keeps them bright, and can even make standard photos and videos brighter. Second, CompressO now works **without opening the app**: from a terminal with the new `compresso` command, and from AI agents such as Claude.

As always, CompressO is free, open-source and completely offline. Here is what is new.

## HDR Boost

Modern screens, like those of recent iPhones, Macs and many laptops, can show highlights far brighter than a normal picture. Photos and videos made for them are called **HDR**, and they look noticeably more vivid.

With **HDR Boost**, CompressO can give a normal photo or video that extra brightness. Turn it on, choose an intensity from 1× to 8×, and the highlights glow on screens that support HDR. On screens that do not, the file simply looks as it did before.

HDR Boost works for images saved as PNG or JPEG XL on macOS, Windows and Linux, and also for JPEG on macOS 15 and later. Those JPEGs keep a standard version inside, so they look right in any viewer.

<Figure label="HDR Boost, at 4.2×" src={hdrBoost} />

## HDR videos stay HDR

Until now, HDR videos, such as those recorded on an iPhone, could come out washed out or banded after compressing. Not anymore: HDR videos, including **Dolby Vision** videos from the iPhone, now stay HDR when they are compressed, at the same brightness and colors.

**HDR Boost** works for videos too, from 1× to 8×. And when a format cannot store HDR, such as GIF, CompressO converts the video to standard brightness with careful tone mapping, so it never looks washed out.

## Remove HDR

Sometimes HDR is not what you want. A very bright photo can look out of place next to normal ones, and some apps and websites do not show HDR correctly.

**Remove HDR** converts HDR photos and videos to standard brightness, with proper tone mapping, so they look natural everywhere. CompressO detects whether a file is HDR and shows the matching setting: HDR Boost for standard files, Remove HDR for HDR ones.

Both settings, including the boost intensity, can be saved in presets and used together with Target Size.

## Straighten

A slightly tilted horizon can spoil a good photo. The new **Straighten** slider in the transform editor tilts photos, videos and animated GIFs by up to 45 degrees either way.

The crop always stays inside the tilted picture, so no empty corners are ever added, and rotating or flipping afterwards keeps the straightened result.

## Compress from the terminal

CompressO now comes with a command line tool, `compresso`. It uses your presets and output settings, as if the files were dropped on the app:

```sh
compresso ~/Movies/clip.mov
```

You can give it several files and whole folders at once, choose a preset for each media type, and save to a folder of your choice:

```sh
compresso photo.jpg clip.mov Trip --video-preset="Web" --output="Compressed"
```

For a single file, the output can also be a file name. A different extension converts the file to that format, with the preset's other settings:

```sh
compresso photo.jpg --output="photo.png"
compresso talk.mp4 --output="talk.mp3"
```

Output settings, such as skipping larger outputs or replacing the original files, follow the app by default, and can be changed for a single command. `--dry-run` shows what would happen without compressing anything, and `--json` and clear exit codes make `compresso` easy to use in scripts.

CompressO runs quietly in the background while it compresses: there is no window, and it quits again once it is done. Every file still shows in the app's queue and history.

### Getting the command

- **macOS**: open Settings › CLI and click **Install**. With Homebrew, the command is installed with the app.
- **Windows**: the installer adds it. Open a new terminal after installing.
- **Linux**: the `.deb` package adds it. For the AppImage, open Settings › CLI and click **Install**.

Run `compresso --help` to see every option, or open **View Examples** in Settings › CLI.

## Let AI agents compress for you

CompressO also includes an **MCP server**, so AI agents such as Claude can compress files for you. Just ask, in your own words:

> Compress the videos in my Downloads folder to MP4 with my Web preset, and save them on the Desktop.

The agent sees your presets, chooses the right settings and tells you what was saved. Everything still happens on your computer, with your own presets.

To set it up, add this to the MCP settings of your agent:

```json
{
  "mcpServers": {
    "compresso": { "command": "compresso", "args": ["mcp"] }
  }
}
```

In Claude Code, it is a single command:

```sh
claude mcp add compresso -- compresso mcp
```

## Bug fixes

This release also fixes a few bugs:

- Compressed HDR videos no longer show banding or wrong brightness, and HDR videos no longer look washed out in GIFs, thumbnails and H.264 output.
- HDR JPEG XL images open and compress correctly, and PNG and JPEG XL outputs keep their HDR colors.
- Copied outputs that are not saved yet are pasted under the name they would be saved with.
- Watched folders no longer miss files in subfolders, in folders moved into them, or in files replaced under a name that was already compressed.

You can find every change in the [changelog](/changelog).
